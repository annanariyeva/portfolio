Engineering shaped by curiosity, adaptability & experience.

My path to engineering has taken me through different countries, languages, cultures, classrooms, and laboratories. Each experience has shaped how I approach unfamiliar problems, work with others, and continue learning.

MY STORY

I grew up without a single place I called home.

I was born in Kazakhstan and spent much of my life moving between countries and cultures. Growing up internationally meant repeatedly entering unfamiliar environments, learning new ways to communicate, and adapting to different communities.

Over time, something that once felt uncertain became one of my greatest strengths. I learned to become comfortable with change, ask questions when I did not understand something, and build connections with people whose experiences were very different from my own.

SOUTH KOREA · AGE 18

Learning to be comfortable being uncomfortable.

At 18, I moved to South Korea on my own to study Korean. I arrived in a country where I was still learning the language and had to navigate everyday life, school, and an entirely new environment independently.

There were plenty of moments when I did not know what to do or how to communicate exactly what I needed. Instead of avoiding those situations, I learned to ask questions, make mistakes, solve problems as they came, and keep moving forward.

That experience gave me much more than language skills. It made me more independent, adaptable, and confident entering environments where I may not immediately know the answer.

FINDING ENGINEERING

Curiosity became something I could build with.

Engineering gave me a way to turn curiosity into something tangible. I became especially interested in problems where design decisions could be tested, measured, improved, and ultimately turned into physical solutions.

Undergraduate research introduced me to that process in practice. Working on ankle-foot orthoses allowed me to move between CAD, manufacturing, mechanical testing, simulation, data analysis, and collaboration with people from different technical backgrounds.

More importantly, the experience taught me that I enjoy engineering most when I can understand the complete process rather than work on one isolated step.

PERSPECTIVE

Engineering doesn't happen in isolation.

My international background has influenced the way I collaborate just as much as the way I approach new environments.

During my undergraduate research, I worked within a project connecting academic research, clinical needs, and an international manufacturing partner in Australia. That experience reinforced the importance of clear communication and understanding the perspectives of people approaching the same problem from different directions.

WHAT'S NEXT

I want to work on engineering that becomes real.

As I continue studying Mechanical Engineering at Georgia Tech, I want to broaden my experience beyond the research environments I already know and work on larger-scale engineering and manufacturing systems.

I am particularly interested in manufacturing, mechanical design, automation, product development, and industries where engineering moves from concept to production.

My goal is to continue learning from experienced engineers while taking on increasingly challenging technical responsibilities and contributing to products and systems that exist beyond the computer screen.
